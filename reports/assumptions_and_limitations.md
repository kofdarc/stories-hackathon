# Assumptions and hypothesis register

| Hypothesis | Supporting evidence | Against / unresolved | Next test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product mix drives Garden gap | Branch baskets differ | Same-product costs also differ; common-mix and Shapley tables quantify residual | Review product contribution bridge |
| Discount policy drives gap | Concessions mechanically reduce contribution | Discount table and Shapley isolate arithmetic; targeting is confounded | Randomize before purchase |
| Lower selling prices | Matched price ratios differ | Demand response unavailable | Controlled product price pilot |
| Procurement contract disadvantage | Same items cost more in Garden | Near-uniform fixed ratios suggest a centralized rule | Match supplier invoices by SKU/date |
| Waste or recipes | Could change ingredient consumption | No waste, recipe or portion fields; fixed recorded costs cannot reveal actual usage | Weigh portions, inspect waste logs |
| Accounting allocation or synthetic rule | Ratios recur across products/months | Records cannot distinguish cash costs from allocations or generated data | Trace cost-master formula and source lineage |

Observed: source revenue, GP, unit costs, quantities, co-purchases, time coverage. Estimated: recoverability, proportional product coverage, constant mix, demand response and incremental bundle adoption. Unknown: avoidable cash costs, elasticity, nonbuyer conversion, supplier quotes, implementation expense, labor and rent. Expense is an editable variable, never an invented measured budget. Gross profit is neither net profit nor free cash flow.

No causal savings or promotion uplift can be inferred. Customer identification is incomplete and potentially selected. Retaining repeated rows preserves the original source; duplicate sensitivity is in the report. Six months across three branches do not establish future demand or seasonality. Financial scenarios are not forecasts and are not added together across overlapping interventions.
